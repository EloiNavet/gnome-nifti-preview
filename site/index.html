GNOME NIfTI Preview

Select a medical image in GNOME Files, press Space, and see the three views, the metadata and the labels — instantly.

Real recording of GNOME Files on Ubuntu 26.04.

Install

wget -qO- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EloiNavet/gnome-nifti-preview/main/get.sh | bash

Paste it in a terminal (Ctrl Alt T). Nothing to compile; everything stays in your home folder, except the program that draws thumbnails in Files, which the installer asks before copying to /usr/local/bin. To remove it: ~/.local/share/gnome-nifti-preview/uninstall.sh

Use

  • SpaceOpen or close the preview
  • ScrollMove through the slices of a view
  • , .4D images: previous or next volume (or drag the slider, or Shift + scroll)
  • [ ]DICOM: previous or next series of the same exam
  • HoverValue under the pointer — label and volume in cm³ on a segmentation
  • ClickNavigate — the other views follow
  • Right-dragBrightness and contrast (double right-click: back to automatic)
  • ← →Previous or next file
  • ONeurological, radiological or stored orientation
  • LColours or grayscale for segmentations and RGB images; real part or magnitude of a spectrum
  • ZUsual range or whole width of a spectrum
  • VChoose the view that Page Up and Page Down scroll, without a mouse

NIfTI-1 and NIfTI-2 (3D to 5D, NIfTI-MRS spectroscopy), FreeSurfer MGH/MGZ, MRtrix, NRRD, MetaImage and DICOM: whole series from a folder, every common compression, DICOMDIR. FreeSurfer colours and names for segmentations, thumbnails in Files, a Preferences window, files on network shares. Works with GNOME Sushi 46 to 51.

In a terminal

The same images over SSH, on a cluster or in a container, without GNOME: nitty.

wget -qO- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EloiNavet/gnome-nifti-preview/main/get.sh | bash -s -- --terminal

One static program in ~/.local/bin (or /usr/local/bin as root), and nothing else. Every format of the preview is read, DICOM series included.

nitty T1w.nii.gz        # the viewer (q quits, ? lists the keys)
nitty -p T1w.nii.gz     # a quick look: the three views, printed

Terminals that draw images (Kitty, Ghostty, WezTerm, iTerm2, Konsole, foot, xterm with Sixel) get real pictures; the others (GNOME Terminal, Ptyxis, the Linux console, tmux) get coloured half-blocks. More in the README.
